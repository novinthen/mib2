## Part A — Material decisions taken (with the alternatives considered)

Each decision records what was chosen, what else was defensible, and why the chosen option is the more conservative or better-evidenced treatment.

### DEC-01 — The MIB RM500 million instrument is a savings seed fund, not a loan scheme
**Chosen:** Describe the MIB 2017 instrument as a RM500 million PNB unit-trust seed fund providing matching booster units to IB40 savers, and rebuild PRG-11 as a matched-savings scheme.
**Rejected:** Carrying forward the source draft's description of a five-year interest-free loan of RM5,000 per household to 100,000 households.
**Why:** MIB 2017 pp.41 and 112 describe a seed fund in terms that admit no loan reading. The loan description, the RM5,000 unit and the 100,000-household figure appear nowhere in the 172-page Blueprint. Carrying an invented instrument into a Cabinet paper would be indefensible. See CNF-007, CLM-012.
**Materiality:** High. It removes a RM500 million headline commitment and the Phase 1 "100,000 households" target built on it.

### DEC-02 — The AS1M equity claim is removed entirely
**Chosen:** Remove the 1.5 billion AS1M units claim, the "equity has gone backwards" narrative, and the Phase 3 successor-fund target.
**Rejected:** Retaining the claim with a qualification.
**Why:** Full-text search of MIB 2017 returns zero matches for the figure in any form. A qualification cannot rescue a figure with no source. Pillar 3 is rebuilt around household savings and asset accumulation, which the Blueprint does support. See CNF-008, CLM-013.
**Materiality:** High. It removes an entire Phase 3 workstream.

### DEC-03 — The 7% civil service target is not carried into MIB 2.0
**Chosen:** Adopt a six-year target range of 4.2%–4.8%, reported alongside an absolute headcount, and record 7% as a longer-horizon aspiration beyond this plan.
**Rejected:** Carrying the MIB's 7% target with a new deadline.
**Why:** Recalculation (ASM-020) shows 7% requires roughly 43,000 net additional Indian officers on a 1,302,429 base — about 7,160 per year for six years. No credible intake assumption supports it. Promising it again would repeat the failure the plan exists to correct.
**Materiality:** High, and politically the most sensitive decision in the package. It is made because a target that cannot be met is worse than a smaller one that can.

### DEC-04 — Ethnic procurement set-asides are removed
**Chosen:** Deliver vendor capability, certification and consortium formation; measure and publish participation; mandate nothing.
**Rejected:** The 2% participation quota and RM100 million contract-value target.
**Why:** An ethnically-defined federal procurement set-aside for a community whose position under Article 153 is that of one of the "other communities" whose legitimate interests are safeguarded, not a community for whom quotas may be reserved carries serious Article 8 exposure and would require Treasury instruction changes and AGC clearance. The second source document independently takes the merit-and-capability position. See CNF-024.
**Materiality:** High.

### DEC-05 — Documentation targets are throughput targets, not clearance guarantees
**Chosen:** Establish a verified caseload in Year 1; then set resolution-rate and cycle-time targets.
**Rejected:** "100% of the residual backlog cleared in 24 months."
**Why:** There is no published residual caseload, the original 25,000 was expressly an estimate, and citizenship determinations under Article 15A, Article 19 and Second Schedule Part II vest the discretion in the Federal Government that no task force can commit to a percentage. See CNF-005.
**Materiality:** High.

### DEC-06 — All electoral and messaging content is excluded
**Chosen:** Remove the electoral-cycle argument from source 1 and the whole of the messaging and voter-persuasion chapters of source 2 (approximately 2,676 words (18.3%)).
**Rejected:** Retaining a condensed political-strategy annex.
**Why:** A submission seeking preliminary Cabinet approval must not contain electoral persuasion material or drafted speeches. The underlying sequencing logic is preserved on administrative grounds. See CNF-019, CNF-030.
**Materiality:** High for document fitness; nil for programme content.

### DEC-07 — Registries are separated
**Chosen:** Keep the documentation caseload system and the household progression record as separate systems with no automatic linkage; make a published Data Protection Impact Assessment a Phase 1 gate.
**Rejected:** The source draft's "consolidated IB40 registry" merging both.
**Why:** Linking citizenship status to welfare eligibility would chill take-up among precisely the households targeted, and the total exclusion of federal and state government processing from the PDPA 2010 under section 3(1) raises rather than lowers the governance obligation. See CNF-032.
**Materiality:** High.

### DEC-08 — Needs-based eligibility throughout
**Chosen:** Define eligibility for every material benefit on income, documentation status or tenure insecurity; target outreach, not entitlement, to the community.
**Rejected:** Ethnicity as an eligibility condition.
**Why:** It is the only construction that is constitutionally robust, and it makes the delivery model extensible to any community facing the same structural barriers — which is also the strongest answer to the "why one community" objection.
**Materiality:** High.

### DEC-09 — Three two-year phases
**Chosen:** Three two-year phases.
**Rejected:** Source 2's executive-summary structure of two phases (Years 1–2, 3–6).
**Why:** Source 2 contradicts itself — its own roadmap chapter uses three phases — and three two-year phases match the locked brief. See CNF-020.

### DEC-10 — Programme volumes are capped below both source drafts
**Chosen:** Cap concurrent household case-management capacity at approximately 60,000 households in Phase 3 (approximately 26% of the 2014-basis low-income Indian household count, or about 10% of all Indian households at peak capacity); preschool at the eligible cohort; housing at approximately 6,000 households. Cumulative navigator reach is not stated before case duration and turnover are observed.
**Rejected:** Universal coverage language ("each participating family", 90% enrolment, 10,000 housing placements).
**Why:** Every capped figure is staffed and costed. Uncapped language is not deliverable and would create the same credibility gap the plan exists to close.

### DEC-11 — Community institution programme is narrowed and made multi-faith
**Chosen:** One religion-neutral safety, accessibility and compliance programme for faith-based and community institutions serving Malaysian Indian communities. The open-ended definition expressly includes Hindu temples, mosques and surau serving Indian Muslim communities, churches, Sikh gurdwaras, Baha'i facilities and other eligible institutions. Common, needs-based criteria apply across faiths; there is no equal allocation or fixed quota by religion. A separate temple and estate-legacy track documents tenure, relocation and registration cases for referral to the competent state authority, while institution-specific coordination follows the relevant legal and religious-administration regime.
**Rejected:** Source 2's Hindu-only national audit; a closed list of four faith categories; federal financing of religious practice; and federal adjudication of land tenure or heritage status.
**Why:** A Malaysian Indian blueprint should not equate Indian identity with Hindu identity, but formal equality cannot erase the distinctive estate-displacement and land-tenure problems affecting temples. The two-track design combines inclusive access with differentiated remedies. Participation requires state, institutional and—where applicable—religious-authority consent. Federal funding is confined to life safety, accessibility, compliance and documented public-interest community-service spaces; it excludes worship, proselytisation, ceremonies, devotional fixtures and doctrinal activity. See CNF-028.

### DEC-12 — No inflation escalation is applied
**Chosen:** State all figures in 2026 prices with no escalation, and disclose that this understates the nominal cash requirement in Years 3–6 by roughly 10–13% at 2.5% annual inflation.
**Rejected:** Applying an assumed deflator.
**Why:** An assumed deflator would embed a Treasury judgement not ours to make. Disclosure is the conservative treatment. See ASM-043, VAL-26.

## Part F — Additional constitutional provisions engaged (added after independent critique)

The earlier legal analysis rested on Article 8 alone. Three further provisions are engaged and
are now on the record:

- **Article 12(1)** — prohibits discrimination on grounds of race in the provision of financial
  aid out of public funds for the education of pupils. Approximately **RM552m** of this portfolio
  (PRG-02, PRG-06, PRG-07) is educational financial aid. This is the reason eligibility for those
  programmes is defined on **household income**, not ethnicity. AGC clearance specifically against
  Article 12(1) is a gating validation item (VAL-30).
- **Article 136** — requires impartial treatment of federal employees of the same grade
  irrespective of race. PRG-13 and PRG-14 are therefore designed as *outreach, preparation and
  publication* measures with **no adjustment to entry standards and no effect on appointment or
  promotion decisions**, which rest with the service commissions.
- **Article 153(1)** — safeguards the special position of the Malays and natives of Sabah and
  Sarawak **and the legitimate interests of other communities**. Malaysian Indians fall within the
  latter limb. This is the correct constitutional framing, and it does not support reserving
  quotas — which is why no quota is proposed.

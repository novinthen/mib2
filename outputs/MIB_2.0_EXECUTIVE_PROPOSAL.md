# MIB 2.0 — Malaysian Indian Progression Blueprint

## A Six-Year National Blueprint for Malaysian Indian Socioeconomic Advancement

### Executive Proposal for Preliminary Consideration by the Prime Minister and Cabinet

---

**Status of this document.** This is an executive proposal seeking *preliminary approval in principle*. It is not a Cabinet Memorandum, not an appropriation request, and not a final implementation blueprint. Preliminary endorsement would authorise validation, detailed design and the preparation of a formal Cabinet submission. It would not constitute an appropriation, statutory authority, procurement approval, or final approval of any individual programme.

**Price basis.** All figures are 2026 nominal ringgit, price base year 2026, with no inflation escalation applied. This understates the nominal cash requirement in Years 3–6 by approximately 10–13% at 2.5% annual inflation, and is disclosed as a limitation (VAL-26).

**Evidence basis.** Every figure in this proposal maps to a claim identifier in `CLAIMS_AND_FIGURES_REGISTER.csv` and to a source or assumption. Figures that could not be verified have been removed, not softened. Where a source draft's figure did not survive inspection, the correction is stated openly in this document rather than buried in an annex.

# 1. Executive proposition

Malaysia's inclusive development framework is needs-based, and it should remain so. Within that framework, one set of structural barriers has proved unusually persistent for Malaysian Indian households: unresolved legal identity, uneven school readiness, a weak school-to-work transition, and negligible asset formation. Each barrier is administratively soluble. Together they compound across a life course, and they have not been closed by successive rounds of funding because no part of government has owned the outcome, published the numbers, or answered for them.

**The proposition is not more money. It is a delivery instrument, an evidence base, and a measured commitment.**

MIB 2.0 proposes:

- **Four pillars** addressing the four structural exclusions, delivered through **sixteen programmes**, each with a named accountable ministry, an accounting officer, a phase, an outcome, a key performance indicator and a cost treatment.
- **Three two-year phases**, gated on evidence rather than on the calendar.
- A **Prime Minister-chaired Task Force** with a delivery secretariat in the Prime Minister's Department, meeting quarterly against a published dashboard — a coordinating and accountability body, not a new authority over ministries.
- A **six-year central cost of RM1,484.273 million**, of which the **incremental new funding requirement is RM847.677 million**, the remaining RM636.596 million being existing allocations and proposed reallocation (CLM-054).

**What is different about this proposal is what it declines to promise.** The MIB's 7% civil service target is not carried forward, because reaching it would require roughly 43,000 net additional Indian officers within six years and is not achievable (CLM-051). No ethnic procurement set-aside is proposed. No citizenship clearance percentage is guaranteed. Eleven of the sixteen indicators have no baseline today, and establishing those baselines — not hitting targets — is the principal deliverable of Phase 1. A plan that promises less and delivers it is the only remaining way to close a credibility gap that a decade of larger promises has widened.

# 3. The reality, stated accurately

## 3.1 What the data show

Malaysian Indians are approximately **6.5% of the population (2024), declining from 6.6% in 2023** (CLM-006). Malaysian Indian households recorded a **mean monthly household income of RM8,950 in 2022**, against a national median of RM6,338 (CLM-004).

**Absolute poverty incidence among Indian households was 5.4% in 2022 — below the Bumiputera rate of 7.9% and above the Chinese rate of 1.9% — having risen from 4.8% in 2019** (CLM-005).

That last sentence is stated first, and plainly, because the case for this proposal does not rest on the claim that this is Malaysia's poorest community. It is not. It rests on three narrower and better-evidenced propositions:

1. **The trend is adverse.** Indian poverty incidence rose between 2019 and 2022 while the community's aggregate income position remained comparatively strong. That divergence is the signature of a widening internal distribution, not of general decline.
2. **Specific exclusions are structural, not incidental.** Unresolved legal identity, in particular, is a barrier that no amount of income growth resolves and that affects no other community at comparable scale.
3. **The measurement gap is itself the problem.** Eleven of the sixteen indicators in this plan have no published baseline. Neither government nor the community can currently establish what has changed.

## 3.2 The community is not homogeneous, and the plan does not treat it as one

A community of roughly 2.2 million people contains a professional and business stratum with incomes well above the national median, and a stratum — concentrated among estate-displaced households in urban peripheries — facing compounded disadvantage. The plan targets the second without characterising the first out of existence.

Material differences the programme design responds to:

- **By documentation status.** Persons without resolved citizenship documentation are excluded from the eKasih welfare register by policy, which means the households in greatest need are structurally invisible to the principal targeting instrument (MIB 2017, p.36). This is the single most consequential distinction in the entire analysis.
- **By life stage.** The barriers facing a five-year-old, a seventeen-year-old and a fifty-year-old are different, and the second source document is correct that government has organised around ministries rather than around this sequence.
- **By household composition.** Elderly applicants, widows, single-parent households and persons with disabilities face procedural barriers that a general programme does not remove (CLM-058).
- **By geography.** Estate-origin, urban-periphery and settled urban populations differ materially. Indian Malaysians in Sabah and Sarawak are not separately analysed in this proposal, and that is recorded as a known analytical gap rather than glossed over.
- **By gender.** Uptake of the AIM PENN financing facility by Indian women demonstrates demand for gender-disaggregated design (CLM-027). This is stated as an observed participation fact, not as a generalisation about household decision-making.

## 3.3 A necessary correction to the source material

Three figures widely used in the drafting material do not survive inspection of the primary Malaysian Indian Blueprint. They are corrected here rather than quietly dropped, because the same figures circulate in public debate:

- The Blueprint's RM500 million instrument was a **PNB unit-trust seed fund providing matching booster units to low-income savers** (MIB 2017, pp.41 and 112). It was **not** a five-year interest-free loan scheme of RM5,000 per household to 100,000 households (CLM-012, **rejected**). No such loan scheme appears anywhere in the Blueprint.
- The claim that **1.5 billion Amanah Saham 1Malaysia units were allocated to the community** does not appear in the Blueprint in any form. Full-text search of all 172 pages returns no matching figure (CLM-013, **unsupported and removed**). The associated narrative that the community "has gone backwards on equity" therefore has no evidential basis and is not advanced.
- The Blueprint's savings target was **at least 50% of registered poor households demonstrating increased savings within five years**, not ten (MIB 2017, pp.33 and 40; CLM-014, **corrected**).

The Blueprint's genuinely verified commitments — 7% civil service participation at all grades within ten years (CLM-008), and resolution of documentation for an **estimated** 25,000 undocumented persons within five years (CLM-010) — are carried forward, one with a reset target and one with a reset method.

# 4. Diagnosis: why funding has not produced structural change

## 4.1 The coordination failure

Both source documents independently reach the same conclusion, and it is consistent with the verified record: **the binding constraint has been delivery, not design or money** (CLM-048). This is labelled an inference. No published programme evaluation establishes causation, and this proposal does not claim one.

The supporting facts are these. The Blueprint's own implementation unit was restructured and relocated more than once (CLM-031). MITRA's allocation rose from approximately RM100 million per year to **RM150 million in 2026** (CLM-019, verified). Over the same period the 7% civil service target moved from 7% aspiration to **3.7% actual as at 31 December 2024** (CLM-007, verified) — that is, funding rose while the flagship outcome did not move.

The structural reason is that the levers sit in different ministries from the money. The documentation caseload sits with the Ministry of Home Affairs. Civil service intake sits with the Public Service Department and, for appointment, with the constitutionally independent Public Service Commission. Matriculation and school condition sit with the Ministry of Education. Housing sits with the Ministry of Housing and Local Government and, for land, with the states. A programme fund cannot direct any of them.

## 4.2 The measurement failure

The second source document's critique is accepted in full and is adopted as this plan's measurement philosophy: *money disbursed, training completed, applications approved and beneficiaries counted are measures of activity, not of whether a household moved forward* (CNF-018).

This has a concrete consequence. Reported beneficiary counts are **reach** — episodes of service — not unique beneficiaries. This proposal never sums reach across programmes, and reports a portfolio unique-beneficiary estimate separately using a stated overlap factor (ASM-030).

## 4.3 The four structural exclusions

| Exclusion | Mechanism | Evidence | Pillar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Legal identity** | Without documents, a person cannot enrol in school, take formal employment, or appear in eKasih — so the most excluded are also the least visible to targeting | MIB 2017 pp.23, 36; CLM-010, CLM-055 | P1 |
| **School readiness** | Children entering Year One behind rarely catch up, and each later intervention costs more and works less | MIB 2017 Pillar 2; CLM-053 | P1 |
| **School-to-work transition** | Training that is not connected to employment, and admissions that assume an informed applicant, both leak capable young people out of the system | Source 2 Tab 3; CLM-038 | P2 |
| **Asset formation** | Income without a buffer means one shock returns a household to crisis; no matched-savings instrument reaches this population | MIB 2017 pp.41, 112 (seed-fund design, never implemented) | P3 |

## 4.4 What this plan does not claim

It does not claim that existing programmes have failed. Verified recent measures — MITRA at RM150 million in 2026 (CLM-019), SJKT maintenance at RM50 million in 2026 across 528 schools serving 78,501 pupils (CLM-016, CLM-017), automatic matriculation offers to **all 6,717 candidates nationally, across all ethnic groups, scoring 10A or better in SPM 2025** (CLM-025), and the AIM PENN facility (CLM-027) — are real. MIB 2.0 runs on these rails; it does not replace them.

Nor does it claim a verified total for existing Indian-community funding. The figure of RM220 million in Budget 2026, used in the source material as the basis of its entire Phase 1 request, **could not be substantiated** (CLM-018). Confirming the true existing envelope is a gating validation item (VAL-03), and the Phase 1 request cannot be finalised until the Ministry of Finance provides it.

# 5. Architecture: pillars, programmes and phases

## 5.1 Theory of change

> **Structural barrier removed → capability built → income stabilised → asset accumulated → dependence reduced.**

Four barriers, removed in sequence, with a single consented household record so that clearing one does not strand the next. The chain is the plan; the programmes are its links.

## 5.2 Strategic principles

1. **Needs-based eligibility.** Every material benefit is defined on household income, documentation status or tenure insecurity. Outreach — not entitlement — is targeted to the community. This is the only constitutionally robust construction and it makes the model extensible to any community facing the same barriers.
2. **Complementarity.** Every programme runs on an existing delivery agency. Only the delivery secretariat and the navigator cadre are new establishment.
3. **Evidence gating.** Where no baseline exists, the Year 2 target *is* the baseline. No numeric outcome target is set on an unmeasured quantity.
4. **Bounded promises.** Volumes are capped at what is staffed and costed.
5. **Published by default.** Every indicator is published, disaggregated, whether or not it flatters.

## 5.3 The pillars and the sixteen programmes

### Pillar 1 — Foundations: identity, early childhood, safe schools

**PRG-01 National Documentation Resolution Programme** — *Ministry of Home Affairs (JPN)* — Phases 1–3
One-stop JPN–JPM desks in every state and federal territory, mobile registration teams to former estate settlements and urban-poor localities, and active case management to resolution. Priority order: children of school age, students, persons with disabilities, elderly applicants, widows and single-parent households (CLM-058).
*First deliverable is a verified caseload baseline within nine months.* Targets are resolution-rate and cycle-time targets, not approval targets: citizenship determination under Article 15A, Article 19 and the Second Schedule Part II of the Federal Constitution is a Ministerial discretion, and no task force may commit it to a percentage. The source draft's "100% of the backlog in 24 months" is rejected on both counts (CLM-034).
**Outcome:** fewer Malaysian-born persons of Indian origin without resolved documentation status; shorter median cycle time. **Six-year central cost: RM26.1 million.**

**PRG-02 Early Childhood Access Programme** — *Ministry of Education* — Phases 1–3
Enrolment fee subsidy, transport and meal support, delivered through existing registered preschool platforms with SJKT and community organisations as the referral channel. Eligibility is household income in the bottom 40%, not ethnicity.
The eligible population is approximately **16,000 children aged 4–5 in low-income Indian households** (CLM-053), derived from cohort size. The source draft's target of 40,000 subsidised places was scaled from a *household* denominator and exceeds the eligible child cohort (CLM-037); it is recalculated. Participation rises from approximately 40% to 80% of the eligible cohort across the three phases.
**Outcome:** higher preschool participation and improved school readiness at Year One entry. **Six-year central cost: RM255.360 million** — the largest single programme in the portfolio.

**PRG-03 SJKT Safety and Learning Environment Programme** — *Ministry of Education* — Phases 1–3
A condition audit of **all 528 SJKT** (CLM-016), a published remediation schedule prioritised strictly by audited safety risk, safety-critical works, digital learning facilities and a trained coordinator per school.
The national dilapidated-schools programme is treated as **existing national funding**; MIB 2.0 claims no part of it as community funding, and funds only the audit, the digital layer and the incremental safety gap (CLM-030). The assumed 55% existing-funding share is provisional and gating (VAL-11).
**Outcome:** every SJKT meets a defined safety and learning-environment standard. **Six-year central cost: RM133.056 million.**

**PRG-04 Multi-Faith Community Institution Safety and Compliance Programme** — *Ministry of National Unity, delivered through JPNIN* — Phases 1–3
One religion-neutral programme for faith-based and community institutions serving Malaysian Indian communities. Eligible institutions include Hindu temples, mosques and surau serving Indian Muslim communities, churches, Sikh gurdwaras, Baha'i facilities and other qualifying institutions; the definition remains open so smaller faith communities are not excluded by omission. A common facility track provides safety, accessibility and regulatory-compliance audits, matched-funding grants for safety-critical works, and compliance advice. Eligibility is based on documented community service, need and lawful institutional consent—not a fixed allocation by religion.

A differentiated temple and estate-legacy track documents land-tenure, relocation and registration issues for referral to the competent state authority; it does not create federal adjudicative power. Institution-specific coordination is conducted with the relevant trustees, representative bodies and religious authorities. **Participation requires the consent of the state, the institution and any relevant religious authority, facility by facility.** Federal support is confined to life safety, accessibility, regulatory compliance and spaces used for documented education, welfare, youth, elderly, cultural or other public-interest services. It cannot finance worship, proselytisation, ceremonies, devotional fixtures or doctrinal activity. Safety works necessary to protect life and public access may be supported regardless of faith, subject to those boundaries. The source draft's national temple audit including land-tenure and heritage adjudication is narrowed and made multi-faith accordingly (CLM-044).

**Outcome:** participating institutions meet defined safety and accessibility standards while distinct temple and estate-legacy cases enter a documented state-referral pathway. **Six-year central cost: RM44.400 million (provisional).** The existing 1,200-facility planning assumption is not a verified multi-faith baseline; the facility map, faith-type distribution, delivery partners and fiscal envelope must be recalibrated before formal approval (ASM-C04, VAL-13). This remains the weakest mandate in the portfolio and is flagged as such (RSP-04).

### Pillar 2 — Progression: education to employment

**PRG-05 School-to-Pathway Guidance and Placement Support** — *Ministry of Education* — Phases 1–3
Structured career profiling in Forms 4–5, admissions clinics for every post-school pathway, and follow-through to placement confirmation. **Six-year central cost: RM19.4 million.**

**PRG-06 TVET and STEM Progression Pipeline** — *Ministry of Human Resources* — Phases 1–3
Training seats in existing accredited institutions, a completion stipend, employer-linked apprenticeships, and mandatory tracking from intake through certification to twelve months of employment. Approximately **30,000 cumulative enrolees** across six years.
The employment threshold is **the prevailing national minimum wage plus 25%, restated and published annually.** The source draft's threshold of RM2,672 per month is a 2014 B40 mean and is not a valid 2026 wage floor (CLM-038). Completion and employment targets are set below the source draft's 85% and 70% because those figures had no evidential basis.
**Outcome:** certified completion and employment above the indexed threshold twelve months after certification. **Six-year central cost: RM279.0 million.**

**PRG-07 Tertiary Access Navigation and Transparency** — *Ministry of Education* — Phases 1–3
Publication of an annual disaggregated intake and offer series, applicant navigation support, and a pathway-adequacy review tabled to the Task Force.
**No additional matriculation seats are requested and none are costed.** The 90:10 framework is unchanged. This is deliberate: two irreconcilable figures for Indian matriculation seats circulate publicly — 1,116 asserted by an advocacy organisation and 1,537 asserted in the source material — and neither is traceable to the Ministry of Education (CLM-023, CLM-024). A gazetted 3.72% sub-quota could not be established as an administrative instrument. The honest first step is publication, not a seat-count target built on a contested number.
**Outcome:** qualified applicants are not lost for informational or procedural reasons, and the actual constraint becomes visible. **Six-year central cost: RM18.0 million.**

**PRG-08 Professional Entry and Mentoring** — *Ministry of Higher Education* — Phases 2–3
Structured mentoring, professional-association placement partnerships and entry-examination preparation. **Six-year central cost: RM8.0 million.**

### Pillar 3 — Livelihoods, enterprise and assets

**PRG-09 Household Progression Programme** — *Prime Minister's Department (delivery secretariat)* — Phases 1–3
A designated Progression Navigator per enrolled household at a caseload of 150, a single consent-based household progression record, coordinated referral to existing programmes, and periodic review. Approximately **120 navigators and 18,000 households in Phase 1, rising to approximately 400 navigators and 60,000 households cumulatively** — approximately 26% of the 2014-basis low-income Indian household count, or about 10% of all Indian households.
Coverage is **capped and is not universal.** The second source document's "each participating family" is given a defined meaning: households enrolled in this programme, not all Indian households (CLM-043). Navigators coordinate access; they do not approve benefits.
**Outcome:** enrolled households show measurable movement in two or more of income stability, employment, savings and housing security. **Six-year central cost: RM133.2 million.**

**PRG-10 Microenterprise Growth and Vendor Capability Programme** — *Ministry of Entrepreneur and Cooperatives Development* — Phases 2–3
Structured advisory on financial management, compliance, digitalisation, quality certification and procurement readiness; consortium formation; bid preparation. Approximately 6,000 enterprises.
**No new financing facility is created.** Financing continues on existing TEKUN, SPUMI, AIM and BRIEF-i rails. **The ethnic procurement set-aside and 2% participation quota in the source material are rejected** on Article 8 equality grounds and would in any case require Treasury instruction changes and AGC clearance (CLM-039). Procurement participation is **measured and published, not mandated.**
**Outcome:** revenue and employment growth among participating enterprises. **Six-year central cost: RM36.0 million.**

**PRG-11 Household Savings and Asset Accumulation Scheme** — *Ministry of Finance* — Phases 1–3
Matched savings capped at RM1,000 per household per year, with the matching ratio varying by income band and a minimum lock-in, rising to approximately 50,000 participating households.
This is structured on the Blueprint's **actual** design — a seed fund providing matching booster units in a qualifying savings product (MIB 2017, pp.41, 112). It is a **new instrument modelled on an MIB design that was never implemented, not the revival of a lapsed scheme.** It requires Ministry of Finance design approval and, if PNB is the vehicle, **PNB's own commercial agreement, which Cabinet cannot direct** (VAL-19).
**Outcome:** more participating households hold a defined minimum financial buffer. **Six-year central cost: RM200.0 million.** Confidence class: **Provisional.**

**PRG-12 Housing Access and Estate Legacy Resolution** — *Ministry of Housing and Local Government* — Phases 1–3
An estate-displacement legacy review producing an actionable administrative record; facilitated access to existing affordable-housing and rent-to-own programmes; deposit and transaction assistance; targeted site infrastructure. Approximately **6,000 households** over six years.
**Housing units are funded through existing national and state housing programmes and are not costed here.** Legacy land and compensation grievances cannot be resolved federally — the review produces a record for referral to states, not a federal determination. Volumes are set well below the source draft's 5,000 by Year 4 and 10,000 by Year 6 because delivery depends on state capacity.
**Outcome:** more assisted households in secure tenure. **Six-year central cost: RM78.0 million.**

### Pillar 4 — Institutional participation

**PRG-13 Public Service Application Pipeline Programme** — *Public Service Department (JPA)* — Phases 1–3
Career awareness through schools, universities and community networks; application guidance; examination and interview preparation; mentoring by serving officers; and **annual publication of a grade-disaggregated composition series.**

The design follows the evidence. The Public Service Department states that the composition gap is driven principally by **very low application volumes from non-Malay candidates, who nonetheless record the highest appointment success rates each year** (CLM-009). The constraint is at application, not at selection. The source material's implication that panel composition is the binding constraint is not supported, and panel measures are retained only as a transparency measure.

**The MIB's 7% target is not carried forward.** On a base of 1,302,429 officers (excluding the Royal Malaysia Police and the Armed Forces), 3.7% is approximately 48,200 officers; 7.0% would be approximately 91,170 — a requirement of roughly **43,000 net additional Indian officers, or about 7,160 per year for six years** (CLM-051). No credible intake assumption supports it. The adopted six-year target is a range of **4.2%–4.8%**, equivalent to approximately 6,500–14,300 net additional officers (CLM-052), reported alongside an absolute headcount because the denominator will itself change (ASM-021). 7% is recorded as a longer-horizon objective beyond this plan.
**Outcome:** measurable, published increase in participation. **Six-year central cost: RM21.0 million** — the highest ratio of visibility to cost in the portfolio.

**PRG-14 Public Service Professional Advancement** — *Public Service Department (JPA)* — Phases 2–3
Access to existing INTAN and JPA development programmes, with participation and grade-band distribution published. Promotion decisions remain with the service commissions. **Six-year central cost: RM8.0 million.**

# 6. Governance and delivery

## 6.1 The Task Force

A **Task Force on Malaysian Indian Progression**, established by Cabinet decision, chaired personally by the Prime Minister, meeting **quarterly without exception**, with a delivery secretariat of approximately 35 posts in the Prime Minister's Department.

**What it is:** a coordinating, accountability and escalation body. It sets service standards, reviews performance, arbitrates inter-ministry blockages, and publishes.

**What it is not:** the source material describes the body as the "legal successor" to the MIB agenda with apex authority over MITRA and PTMI. **That description is not adopted** (CLM-042). A Cabinet decision cannot effect a statutory transfer of functions. MITRA remains an executing agency with its own accounting officer. **Every programme retains its accountable ministry and its accounting officer**, and the Task Force holds no programme funds.

This preserves the second source document's principle — *education remains responsible for education; housing for housing* — while supplying the convening authority that the first correctly identifies as the missing element. The justification for Prime Ministerial chairmanship is specific: the levers sit in at least six ministries plus the states and a constitutionally independent service commission, and no ministerial-level body can convene that table.

## 6.2 The five delivery principles

Adopted from the second source document and given operational definition:

| Principle | Operational meaning | Programme |
|---|---|---|
| **One front door** | A single entry point per state that routes rather than replaces | PRG-01, PRG-09 |
| **One family profile** | A single **consent-based** household record, legally separated from the documentation caseload system | PRG-09 |
| **One progression navigator** | A named case officer at a caseload of 150 households | PRG-09 |
| **One progression pathway** | Referral chains defined so that clearing one barrier triggers the next step | All |
| **One public dashboard** | Published within 14 days of each quarterly meeting | PRG-16 |

## 6.3 Accountability

- **Quarterly** Task Force review; dashboard published within 14 days **whether or not the meeting is held**, so non-convening is itself visible.
- **Annually**, a public report and an Auditor-General audit tabled in Parliament.
- **At end-Year 3**, an independent mid-term evaluation that gates Phase 3.
- **In Year 6**, an independent final evaluation.
- **Procurement** follows standard Treasury instructions with no exemption sought; award lists and the prioritised remediation schedule published in advance so departures from audited priority order are visible.

| **Benchmarked** — based on comparable official programmes or credible unit-cost evidence | RM1109.873m | 31.9% |

**No cost line in this portfolio is Confirmed.** That is stated plainly because it determines what Cabinet is being asked to approve: a framework in principle, not a costed programme. No programme in this proposal is described as fully costed while material assumptions remain provisional.

## 7.3 Cost structure

Costs distinguish **one-off** (audits, systems, evaluations, capital works) from **recurring** (subsidies, stipends, navigator salaries, matched savings). Categories are development expenditure, operating expenditure, transfers and grants, administration (4.0% of delivery) and contingency (7.5% of delivery plus administration). Contingency is set at the lower end of the 5–15% range because the portfolio is weighted towards recurrent service delivery; a higher rate would be appropriate if the PRG-03 and PRG-12 capital scopes expand.

## 7.4 Cash-flow and displacement

Phase costs rise across the six years (RM355.3m → RM520.8m → RM608.2m), which is deliberate: Phase 1 is dominated by baseline-setting rather than by scaled delivery. **Phase gating prevents unfunded commitments** — Phase 2 scope is authorised only after the Phase 1 evaluation, and no Phase 3 commitment is entered before Year 5 appropriation. Each phase is separately costed, so a phase can be scaled down without stranding earlier investment.

The principal displacement risk is not fiscal but administrative: delivery capacity in lead ministries is already committed (RSK-09). This is why every programme runs on an existing agency and why volumes are capped below both source drafts.

## 7.5 Distributional and equity tests

- **Geographic concentration** (RSK-12): all indicators are disaggregated by state; the dashboard reports geographic distribution so concentration is visible within one quarter.
- **Exclusion of the informal** (RSK-13): needs-based eligibility using documented income would exclude the most vulnerable. Alternative means-assessment pathways — community verification and social-welfare officer assessment — are built in, and documentation status is expressly **not** a barrier to PRG-01 or PRG-02.
- **Perverse incentives** (RSK-14): graduation is defined by sustained movement over 24 months, not by exit from assistance; re-entry carries no penalty; the independent evaluation specifically tests for premature exit.
- **Leakage** (RSK-11): payments to institutions and verified accounts rather than cash where possible; published recipient lists above a threshold; the secretariat holds no programme funds.
- **Double counting** (RSK-15): reach is never summed across programmes; unique beneficiaries are reported separately with a stated overlap factor.

# 10. Cabinet objections, answered

**1. Why a targeted blueprint within a needs-based national policy?**
Because the barriers are structural and specific, not general. Eligibility here is needs-based — income, documentation status, tenure insecurity. What is targeted is *outreach*, not entitlement. And the largest single barrier, unresolved legal identity, affects no other community at comparable scale. Note also what this proposal does **not** argue: Indian absolute poverty (5.4%, 2022) is below the Bumiputera rate (7.9%). The case is structural exclusion and an adverse trend, not relative deprivation.

**2. How does this avoid duplicating existing institutions and programmes?**
Every programme runs on an existing delivery agency; only the secretariat and navigator cadre are new establishment. Each programme carries an explicit duplication assessment in `PROGRAMME_REGISTER.csv`. Four proposed measures were removed as duplicative or unlawful. MITRA continues as an executing agency and is not superseded.

**3. Why should the Prime Minister chair?**
Because the levers sit in at least six ministries, the states, and a constitutionally independent service commission. No ministerial-level body can convene that table. This is also the plan's largest risk (RSK-01), and Cabinet should treat the chairmanship commitment as the substantive question, not a formality.

**4. What is new funding versus existing or redirected?**
Of RM1,484.273 million gross, RM847.677 million is new; RM636.596 million is existing allocation and proposed reallocation. The new requirement averages approximately RM141 million per year. The true existing envelope is **not yet verified** — the RM220 million figure in circulation could not be substantiated (CLM-018, VAL-03).

**5. Are ministries capable of delivery within six years?**
Not at the volumes the source material proposed. Volumes have been cut to what is staffed and costed: household case management capped at approximately 26% of the 2014-basis low-income Indian household count, housing at 6,000 households, preschool at the actual child cohort. Delivery capacity — not money — is the binding constraint (RSK-09).

**6. How will beneficiary selection, leakage and overlap be controlled?**
Published eligibility criteria; payment to institutions and verified accounts; published recipient lists; Auditor-General audit tabled in Parliament; the secretariat holds no funds. Overlap is modelled explicitly with a stated factor and reach is never summed across programmes.

**7. What measurable outcomes justify the expenditure?**
Sixteen indicators with defined verification sources. Honestly: eleven have no baseline, so for those the first measurable outcome is a published baseline. Cabinet is entitled to regard that as a modest return for Phase 1, and it is the accurate position.

**8. What if data or performance are weaker than assumed?**
Phase gating. The independent mid-term evaluation at end-Year 3 publishes a pass/correct/re-scope verdict on every indicator before Phase 3 funds flow. The conservative scenario (RM1,158.487m) is the fallback operating envelope. Individual programmes — notably PRG-11 at RM200m — can be discontinued at a defined decision point without stranding the rest.

**9. What requires endorsement now, and what remains subject to validation?**
Endorsement now: direction, architecture, governance and the fiscal framework in principle. Subject to validation: twenty-eight items in `ASSUMPTIONS_AND_DECISIONS.md` Part C, four of them gating. No appropriation is sought. Phase 2 and Phase 3 envelopes are expressly not sought.

**10. How will progress be independently measured and publicly reported?**
Quarterly published dashboards; annual public report; annual Auditor-General audit tabled in Parliament; independent mid-term and final evaluations published in full. Publication is committed in advance so that selective disclosure is not available later.

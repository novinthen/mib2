# Stage 1 — Comparative Diagnostic and Unified Architecture

---

## A. Executive diagnostic

### Source 1 — *A 6-Year Plan of Action for the Socio-Economic, Educational and Political Upliftment of the Malaysian Indian Community* (≈10,000 words)

**Central argument.** The Malaysian Indian Blueprint of 2017 was a sound diagnosis that was never delivered, because no body at the apex of government owned the outcomes. The community therefore needs not a new blueprint but an execution engine: a Task Force under the Prime Minister's Department, chaired personally by the Prime Minister, meeting quarterly against a published dashboard.

**Principal strengths.** It is specific where policy documents are usually vague. It carries baselines, named owners, deadlines and a consolidated eleven-row scorecard. It credits the current administration's record rather than attacking it, which is both fair and tactically sound. Its institutional diagnosis — that delivery units placed in line ministries lose convening power over other ministries — is correct and is the single most useful idea in either document.

**Principal weaknesses.** Its evidence base does not survive inspection. Three of its load-bearing figures are wrong or unsupported: the MIB's RM500 million instrument was a savings seed fund, not an interest-free loan scheme (CLM-012); the 1.5 billion AS1M unit allocation appears nowhere in the Blueprint (CLM-013); and the RM220 million Budget 2026 envelope on which the entire Phase 1 funding request rests could not be substantiated (CLM-018). Its baselines are 2014-vintage figures presented as current. Its targets are mostly undeliverable — 7% civil service participation would need roughly 43,000 net additional officers. It contains no costing at all beyond scattered references. And it frames the whole plan around the electoral cycle, which disqualifies it as a Cabinet submission in its present form.

**Contribution to the final proposal.** The governance architecture, the phase logic, the discipline of named owner–deadline–budget line, and the completion-agenda posture. Its specificity is retained; its figures are not.

### Source 2 — *MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees* (≈14,700 words)

**Central argument.** The problem is not that assistance is absent but that it is fragmented, so families receive help at one life stage and hit a new barrier at the next. Government should therefore organise around the household's journey rather than around ministries, and commit to three guarantees covering childhood, youth and family economic stability.

**Principal strengths.** The best writing in either document, and the better organising idea. The progression frame — vulnerability to stability to mobility to ownership — is a genuine advance on programme-by-programme design. Its critique of measurement is sharp and correct: *"Money is disbursed. Training is completed. An application is approved. A beneficiary is counted. But the system does not consistently answer... Did the person or family move forward?"* It supplies the delivery architecture (one front door, one family profile, one navigator, one pathway, one dashboard) and the heterogeneity that source 1 lacks, naming the elderly applicant, the single mother and the displaced estate family as distinct cases. It explicitly refuses to portray the community only as welfare recipients. And it contributes one programme entirely absent from source 1: the community asset audit.

**Principal weaknesses.** It contains no statistic, baseline, target, cost, owner, timeline or citation anywhere in 14,700 words. Its guarantees are absolute and unqualified. Approximately 18% of it — the whole of chapters 7 and 8 — is political messaging, signature statements, drafted speech text and voter-persuasion logic, which cannot appear in a Cabinet paper. It contradicts itself on the phase structure between its executive summary and its roadmap chapter. And it repeats its central device many times over: the "visible delivery" imagery recurs at least six times in near-identical form.

**Contribution to the final proposal.** The narrative spine, the delivery architecture, the measurement philosophy, the heterogeneity, the anti-stigmatisation posture, and one programme.

### The two documents are complementary in an unusually clean way

Source 1 has numbers without a narrative that survives scrutiny; source 2 has a narrative without numbers. Source 1 has governance; source 2 has delivery. Source 1 treats the community as an aggregate defined by its poorest segment; source 2 treats it as a set of households at different life stages. Neither is adequate alone, and neither can be adopted as drafted.

### The most consequential risks in integration

1. **Evidence risk (critical).** Source 1's figures carry an authority they have not earned. Every material figure had to be re-verified against the primary MIB 2017 text and against DOSM. Five did not survive.
2. **Legality risk (critical).** Ethnic procurement set-asides, a "legal successor" body with directive authority over ministries, a merged citizenship-and-welfare registry, and a citizenship clearance guarantee are each legally unsafe.
3. **Deliverability risk (critical).** The headline targets in source 1 are not achievable, and source 2's guarantees are unbounded.
4. **Framing risk (major).** The evidence does not support the implicit claim that this is the country's poorest community. DOSM records Indian absolute poverty at 5.4% in 2022 against 7.9% for Bumiputera. The case must be rebuilt on structural exclusion and adverse trend, not on relative deprivation — and stating this plainly is the only durable answer to the fairness objection.
5. **Fitness risk (critical).** Roughly a third of source 2 and the strategic spine of source 1 are political rather than administrative documents.

## G. Proposed unified architecture

**Four pillars plus a cross-cutting delivery layer.** The pillars are derived from the intersection of the MIB's four thrusts, source 2's three guarantees and the life-stage logic. No pillar is retained merely because it appears in a source document — the MIB's "Increasing Social Inclusion" is reconstructed as institutional participation, because that is what the evidence actually supports.

| Pillar | Guarantee it serves | Programmes |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Foundations: identity, early childhood, safe schools** | No child starts behind | PRG-01 Documentation; PRG-02 Early childhood; PRG-03 SJKT safety; PRG-04 Community facilities |
| **P2 — Progression: education to employment** | No young person stalls after school | PRG-05 Pathway guidance; PRG-06 TVET/STEM; PRG-07 Tertiary access transparency; PRG-08 Professional entry |
| **P3 — Livelihoods, enterprise and assets** | No family stays trapped in instability | PRG-09 Household progression; PRG-10 Enterprise capability; PRG-11 Savings and assets; PRG-12 Housing |
| **P4 — Institutional participation** | The state reflects the nation | PRG-13 Public service pipeline; PRG-14 Professional advancement |
| **X — Delivery, data and accountability** | Cross-cutting | PRG-15 Task Force and secretariat; PRG-16 Data, evaluation, reporting |

**Theory of change.** Structural exclusion at four points — legal identity, school readiness, the school-to-work transition, and asset formation — compounds across a life course and across generations. Removing each barrier in sequence, with a single household record so that clearing one does not strand the next, converts episodic assistance into cumulative progression. The chain runs: *barrier removed → capability built → income stabilised → asset accumulated → dependence reduced.*

**Phase logic.** Phase 1 (Years 1–2) establishes what does not exist: verified baselines, the delivery machinery, and the measures that need only administrative action. Phase 2 (Years 3–4) builds the pathways that require Phase 1's foundations. Phase 3 (Years 5–6) consolidates and graduates. The sequencing is justified administratively, not electorally: measures requiring no new legislation or appropriation come first.

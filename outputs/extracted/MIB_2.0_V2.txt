### Executive Summary
## MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees
### A Six-Year National Action Plan for Malaysian Indian Progression, Mobility and Shared Prosperity
#### From Vulnerability to Stability. From Stability to Mobility. From Mobility to Ownership.
Malaysia succeeds when every community has a genuine opportunity to contribute, compete and prosper. The Malaysian Indian community has contributed immensely to nation-building for generations, yet too many families continue to face barriers that prevent them from fully participating in the country's economic and social progress. These barriers are rarely isolated. A child without proper documentation struggles to enter school. A student without guidance misses educational opportunities. A young graduate without networks struggles to secure quality employment. A family living with unstable income postpones home ownership, business growth and long-term wealth creation.
Government assistance has existed for many years, and successive administrations have introduced programmes that have benefited many Malaysians, including the Indian community. Under the MADANI Government, important progress has already been made through increased allocations for community development, expanded educational opportunities, entrepreneurship financing, improvements to Tamil school infrastructure and renewed attention towards long-standing documentation issues. These efforts represent important building blocks that deserve recognition.
However, despite these initiatives, many Malaysian Indians continue to experience government support as a series of separate programmes rather than as a continuous pathway towards long-term stability and upward mobility. Families often receive assistance at one stage of life only to encounter new barriers at the next. Success depends too heavily on navigating multiple agencies, understanding complex application processes, and knowing where to seek help. As a result, assistance frequently solves an immediate problem without changing a family's long-term trajectory.
The challenge facing Malaysia today is therefore not simply whether assistance exists. The challenge is whether assistance creates progression.
This proposal introduces a fundamentally different national approach.
Rather than focusing on individual programmes, grants or one-off initiatives, it establishes a clear progression framework that accompanies Malaysian Indian families throughout the most critical stages of life. The objective is not merely to provide assistance, but to ensure that assistance consistently leads to greater opportunity, higher incomes, stronger educational outcomes and long-term asset ownership.
At the heart of this proposal are three national commitments that every Malaysian Indian family can easily understand and measure.
## The Three MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees
### Guarantee One
### No Indian-born Child Gets Stuck at the Starting Line
Every Malaysian-born child deserves to begin life with identity, education, nutrition and opportunity.
This guarantee focuses on ensuring that children are never disadvantaged because of documentation issues, limited preschool access, inadequate school readiness or poor educational infrastructure. Every child should enter primary school prepared to learn, supported by safe schools and connected to the opportunities necessary for future success.
### Guarantee Two
### No Indian Youth Gets Stuck After School
Education must lead somewhere.
Every young Malaysian Indian should have a visible pathway into higher education, technical training, professional careers, entrepreneurship or public service. No capable young person should complete school only to become disconnected from employment or opportunity.
The Government's responsibility is not only to provide educational access, but to ensure that education leads to meaningful careers and upward social mobility.
### Guarantee Three
### No Indian Family Stays Trapped in Low-Income Instability
Government support should not become permanent dependence.
Every family should have access to a structured pathway towards financial stability through stable employment, entrepreneurship, affordable housing, savings, business growth and asset ownership.
The ultimate goal is not simply to reduce hardship, but to enable families to build lasting security and pass greater opportunities to the next generation.
These guarantees transform the conversation from assistance to progression.
Instead of asking, "How many programmes were launched?", Malaysians will ask:
- How many undocumented children obtained their identity?
- How many students successfully entered higher education?
- How many young people secured quality employment?
- How many families moved from low-income vulnerability into financial stability?
- How many first-time homeowners and business owners emerged?
- How many households built savings and long-term assets?
This shift represents a new standard of public accountability—measuring real life outcomes rather than administrative activity.
## A Six-Year Roadmap for Visible Change
The proposal is deliberately structured across six years because meaningful socio-economic transformation requires both immediate action and sustained investment.
### Phase One (Years 1–2): Deliver Visible Results
The first phase focuses on solving problems that families can immediately see and experience.
Priority actions include:
- accelerating documentation and citizenship resolution;
- improving access to education and preschools;
- repairing essential infrastructure in Tamil schools;
- creating structured pathways into the civil service;
- expanding access to entrepreneurship support and household financial assistance;
- addressing estate legacy housing issues;
- and launching a nationwide audit and restoration programme for Hindu temples and community assets as centres of education, culture and social services.
These are practical actions capable of producing measurable improvements within two years.
The objective is simple:
Families should be able to say that government has not merely announced new initiatives—it has solved real problems affecting their daily lives.
### Phase Two (Years 3–6): Build Permanent Progress
Once immediate barriers have been removed, the focus shifts towards creating permanent structures for long-term prosperity.
This includes:
- integrated education-to-employment pathways;
- industry-linked TVET and professional development programmes;
- stronger participation in government procurement and SME growth;
- structured poverty graduation rather than prolonged welfare dependence;
- increased savings, investment and home ownership;
- stronger representation within the civil service and public institutions;
- and a consistent delivery model that survives beyond political cycles.
By the end of six years, the measure of success will not be the number of programmes introduced, but the number of families whose lives have fundamentally improved.
# A New Political Compact
This proposal also offers a new political compact between the Government and the Malaysian Indian community.
Rather than asking citizens to trust future promises, the Government commits itself to delivering measurable progress at every stage of life.
It recognises that trust is earned not through speeches but through visible delivery.
It recognises that dignity comes not from dependency but from opportunity.
It recognises that genuine inclusion means ensuring every Malaysian has a realistic pathway towards prosperity.
For Prime Minister Dato' Seri Anwar Ibrahim, this presents an opportunity to define a new chapter in Malaysia's nation-building story.
Not by creating another blueprint.
Not by announcing another collection of disconnected programmes.
But by introducing the country's first integrated progression model for a community that has long sought not special treatment, but a fair opportunity to succeed.
This is a plan that people can understand because it speaks to everyday life.
A child who enters school prepared.
A young person who enters meaningful employment.
A family that moves from insecurity to ownership.
That is the promise of the MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees.
It is a promise built not on rhetoric, but on visible progress, measurable outcomes and shared national prosperity.
It is a plan that allows the Prime Minister to say, with credibility:
"Our responsibility is not merely to provide assistance. Our responsibility is to ensure that every Malaysian Indian child, every young person and every family has a clear pathway to move forward. We are replacing scattered assistance with visible progression. We are building ladders, not lifelines. And we will measure our success not by how much we spend, but by how many lives we help transform."

## Chapter 2: The Core Political Problem
# 2. The Core Political Problem
The central problem facing the Malaysian Indian community is not the complete absence of government assistance.
The problem is that assistance is fragmented, difficult to navigate and rarely organised into a clear pathway towards lasting progress.
A family may receive welfare support but remain trapped in unstable income. A student may obtain school assistance but receive no guidance into higher education or employment. A small trader may receive financing but remain unable to enter larger markets or government supply chains. A young graduate may hold the right qualification but lack a structured route into professional employment or public service.
Each intervention may provide temporary relief. Too often, however, the family remains in the same social and economic position.
This is the political failure that must now be corrected.
## 2.1 People Experience Problems as a Chain, Not as Separate Categories
Government programmes are commonly divided according to ministries, agencies and administrative responsibilities.
Families do not experience life in that way.
A household may simultaneously face:
- unresolved documentation;
- unstable employment;
- poor housing;
- school transport costs;
- inadequate preschool access;
- business debt;
- limited savings;
- and uncertainty about a child’s future.
These problems reinforce one another.
A documentation problem can prevent a child from entering school or a young person from securing formal employment. Low educational attainment can lead to low-wage work. Unstable income can prevent a family from obtaining secure housing. Without housing security or savings, one emergency can return the household to crisis.
When government responds to only one part of this chain, the underlying instability remains.
The family may receive help, but it does not move forward.
The Malaysian Indian community therefore does not need more disconnected points of assistance. It needs a progression pathway that connects the solution to one problem with the next stage of advancement.
## 2.2 Government Help Is Scattered Across Too Many Doors
The existing system requires families to approach different agencies for different needs.
Documentation matters sit with one authority. School placement and education issues sit with another. Skills training, employment, entrepreneurship, housing and welfare are each handled through separate institutions, application processes and eligibility rules.
For a well-connected or professionally advised family, this system may be manageable.
For a low-income household, an elderly applicant, a single mother, a displaced estate family or an undocumented person, it can become an obstacle in itself.
The result is predictable:
- families do not know where to begin;
- applications are delayed or abandoned;
- the same information is repeatedly submitted;
- cases are passed between agencies;
- vulnerable households depend on political intermediaries;
- and assistance often reaches those who can navigate the system rather than those facing the greatest need.
This creates a damaging public impression that help exists on paper but cannot be reliably accessed in real life.
The political consequence is serious. Every unresolved case becomes evidence, in the eyes of the community, that government announcements are not connected to delivery.
## 2.3 Assistance Does Not Consistently Produce Progression
Over the years, substantial attention has been given to allocations, grants, training programmes, school support, welfare assistance and entrepreneurship schemes.
Some have produced genuine benefits and should be continued.
But the public debate has too often focused on how much money was announced, how many programmes were launched or how many applications were received.
These measures do not prove that lives have changed.
A programme is successful only when it produces a clear improvement in the beneficiary’s position.
A training course must lead to certification, placement and higher income.
A business loan must lead to stronger revenue, better compliance, new customers and eventually a larger enterprise.
Housing assistance must move a family towards secure tenure or ownership.
Educational support must lead to progression into further study, training or employment.
Welfare assistance must stabilise a household and help those capable of working move towards independence.
The present weakness is that too many programmes stop at the point of delivery. They do not track what happens next.
Money is disbursed.
Training is completed.
An application is approved.
A beneficiary is counted.
But the system does not consistently answer the most important question:
Did the person or family move forward?
## 2.4 The Community Has Heard Plans Before
The Malaysian Indian community is not suffering from a shortage of policy announcements.
Previous initiatives have identified many of the correct problems: documentation, poverty, educational inequality, Tamil school infrastructure, limited wealth ownership, youth disengagement and under-representation in public institutions.
The source action plan itself recognises that the Malaysian Indian Blueprint provided a broad diagnosis and that the unresolved challenge was implementation, continuity and accountable delivery.
The political problem is therefore not that the Government lacks ideas.
The political problem is that the community has repeatedly seen plans launched without being able to clearly identify what was completed, what failed, who was responsible and what changed in the average household.
This has created a deep trust deficit.
Indian voters no longer respond strongly to broad promises, large headline allocations or the creation of another committee.
They want visible proof.
They want to know:
- Which long-pending documentation cases will be resolved?
- Which schools will be repaired, and by when?
- Which qualified students will receive placements?
- Which young people will enter training and secure jobs?
- Which families will obtain housing?
- Which small businesses will grow beyond subsistence?
- Which households will move out of chronic poverty?
- Which temples and community assets will be made safe, lawful and sustainable?
- Who will answer publicly when delivery fails?
A political plan that cannot answer these questions will be treated as another announcement.
## 2.5 The Community Does Not Want Permanent Dependency
It would be a serious political mistake to frame Malaysian Indians only as recipients of welfare.
The community includes workers, professionals, educators, traders, entrepreneurs, civil servants, cultural leaders, young graduates and families seeking a fair chance to build a better future.
Low-income households require immediate protection, but protection alone is insufficient.
People want stable work.
They want their children to enter good schools and meaningful careers.
They want businesses that can grow.
They want secure housing.
They want savings.
They want assets.
They want representation in institutions that shape the country.
Most importantly, they want to see the next generation move further than the previous one.
The correct political proposition is therefore not permanent aid.
It is a guaranteed route out of instability.
Welfare must become stability.
Stability must become mobility.
Mobility must become ownership.
## 2.6 The Missing Element Is a State-Backed Promise of Movement
Existing programmes provide pieces of help.
What is missing is a single promise that connects those pieces into a progression journey.
At present, no Indian family can clearly see a government-backed pathway that says:
- resolve the child’s documentation;
- secure preschool and school access;
- monitor educational progress;
- guide the student after school;
- connect the youth to training, university, professional work or public service;
- stabilise the household’s income;
- help the family secure housing and savings;
- grow the family business;
- and move the household towards asset ownership.
Without this connection, the Government appears active but not transformative.
The MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees correct this weakness by defining success around movement through life stages rather than participation in programmes.
The guarantees establish a simple public test:
### Is the child moving forward?
### Is the young person moving forward?
### Is the family moving forward?
If the answer is no, government intervention is incomplete.
## 2.7 The Political Risk of Continuing the Existing Approach
If the Government continues with scattered announcements, annual allocations and isolated programmes, several consequences are likely.
First, successful initiatives will not receive the political recognition they deserve because the public will not see them as part of a coherent plan.
Second, implementation gaps will continue to damage trust even where funding has increased.
Third, households facing multiple problems will remain trapped between agencies.
Fourth, political competitors will continue to argue that the Government remembers the community only during elections.
Finally, Indian voters may conclude that changing governments changes programme names but not their lived reality.
This is avoidable.
The Government does not need to promise everything at once. It needs to select concrete problems, solve them visibly and show how each solution leads to the next stage of progress.
## 2.8 The Political Opportunity for Anwar
Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim has an opportunity to make a clear break from the politics of fragmented assistance.
He can define his approach around a simple principle:
Government support must produce visible progression.
This allows the Prime Minister to move beyond the old debate over whether enough has been allocated to the Indian community.
The stronger question is:
What changed because the Government acted?
Under this approach, political credit is earned through outcomes that can be seen in households, schools, workplaces, housing communities, businesses, temples and public institutions.
A child receives documentation and enters school.
A school roof is repaired before it becomes dangerous.
A young person completes training and starts a job.
A graduate enters the civil service.
A mother grows a home business into a registered enterprise.
A displaced family enters a rent-to-own home.
A household begins saving and no longer depends on emergency assistance.
A temple becomes safe, compliant and capable of supporting cultural, educational and social programmes.
These are not abstract policy achievements.
They are politically visible changes in everyday life.
## 2.9 The Core Choice
The choice before the Government is straightforward.
It can continue to distribute assistance through separate programmes and defend its record through allocations and activity figures.
Or it can reorganise those efforts around a national promise of progression and judge itself by whether people actually move forward.
The first approach manages problems.
The second changes lives.
The MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees adopt the second approach.
They replace scattered assistance with a pathway.
They replace temporary relief with measurable advancement.
They replace broad promises with visible household outcomes.
They replace dependency with dignity, mobility and ownership.
This is the core political problem the six-year action plan is designed to solve.

# 3. The Three MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees
The success of this proposal will not be measured by the number of programmes introduced, the size of annual allocations, or the number of agencies involved.
It will be measured by one simple question:
Are Malaysian Indian families moving forward?
For too long, government interventions have been organised around administrative responsibilities rather than around the actual journey of a family.
A child receives assistance from one agency.
A student depends on another.
A graduate enters a completely different system.
A business owner applies elsewhere.
Housing, welfare, healthcare and community development are handled separately.
Every stage begins again.
Every application starts over.
Every family must repeatedly find its own way through government.
This proposal replaces that fragmented experience with three national guarantees that every Malaysian Indian can understand.
These guarantees are not slogans.
They are promises that define the Government's responsibility at the three most important stages of life.
If these guarantees are fulfilled, the next generation of Malaysian Indians will not simply receive more assistance.
They will experience greater opportunity, stronger social mobility and lasting economic security.
# Guarantee One
# No Indian-born Child Gets Stuck at the Starting Line
## What This Guarantee Means
No child chooses the circumstances into which they are born.
Whether a child is born into an estate family, an urban B40 household, a fishing village, a new village or a city apartment should never determine the opportunities available throughout life.
Every Malaysian-born Indian child deserves to begin life with the same basic foundation for success:
- a recognised legal identity;
- access to healthcare;
- early childhood education;
- safe schools;
- quality learning environments;
- and the support necessary to enter primary school ready to succeed.
The Government's responsibility begins long before secondary school or university.
It begins from birth.
When children fall behind during these early years, every subsequent intervention becomes more expensive, more difficult and less effective.
The objective is therefore simple:
Every child should start strong.
## What Government Will Do
The first guarantee commits the Government to building a complete foundation for every child.
Priority actions include:
### Resolve Documentation Early
Every Malaysian-born child facing documentation or nationality issues will receive priority case management with dedicated resolution pathways.
No child should lose years of education because identity documents remain unresolved.
### Expand Preschool Access
Children from low-income households will have greater access to quality preschool education supported by transport assistance, nutritional support where needed and active enrolment outreach.
The focus is not merely increasing enrolment.
The objective is ensuring every child enters primary school prepared to learn.
### Strengthen Tamil School Foundations
Tamil schools remain an important educational institution for many Malaysian Indian families.
Every school should provide children with an environment that is safe, modern and conducive to learning.
Priority improvements include:
- structural repairs;
- clean toilets;
- reliable water supply;
- safe electrical systems;
- digital classrooms;
- internet connectivity;
- science and learning facilities;
- and improved learning environments.
Children should not have to overcome poor infrastructure before they even begin learning.
### Build Stronger Community Support
Families often receive their earliest support through community organisations, temples, local volunteers and civil society.
Rather than treating these institutions as symbolic cultural spaces, the Government will recognise and strengthen their role in supporting:
- early childhood development;
- parenting education;
- after-school learning;
- nutrition programmes;
- language and cultural education;
- counselling;
- youth engagement;
- and community volunteering.
The goal is to ensure that every child grows within a supportive local ecosystem rather than relying solely on government agencies.
## What Families Will Feel
Parents will experience faster problem resolution instead of years of uncertainty.
Children will enter school with confidence rather than disadvantage.
Schools will become visibly safer and better equipped.
Communities will see tangible investment in places that directly shape children's futures.
Instead of worrying about whether their child can begin the journey, families can focus on helping that child succeed.
## What Success Looks Like
Within six years:
- documentation delays affecting children become the exception rather than the norm;
- preschool participation among low-income Indian children rises significantly;
- Tamil schools are known for safe, modern learning environments;
- children enter primary school better prepared;
- and educational inequality begins to narrow before it becomes permanent.
The first guarantee ensures that no child's future is limited before their education has even begun.
# Guarantee Two
# No Indian Youth Gets Stuck After School
## What This Guarantee Means
Finishing school should represent the beginning of opportunity, not the beginning of uncertainty.
Yet many young Malaysians experience the opposite.
Students complete SPM but have little understanding of available pathways.
Some qualify for higher education but struggle to navigate admissions.
Others enter low-skilled employment despite having greater potential.
Some simply disappear from the education and employment system altogether.
The nation cannot afford to lose talent because young people lack direction.
Every Malaysian Indian youth should know that there is a clear pathway after school.
Not just education.
Not just training.
A pathway into a productive career.
## What Government Will Do
The second guarantee creates a national progression system from school into adulthood.
### Career Profiling Before Graduation
Students will receive structured career guidance before completing secondary education.
They should understand:
- their strengths;
- potential career options;
- emerging industries;
- education pathways;
- and employment opportunities.
Career planning should begin before students leave school—not after they become unemployed.
### Clear Higher Education Pathways
Students pursuing universities, matriculation, polytechnics, community colleges or technical education should receive coordinated admissions support.
Qualified students should not lose opportunities simply because they lack information or guidance.
### Industry-Linked TVET
Technical education will be directly connected to employers.
Training must lead to employment.
Every programme should be designed with industry participation so that graduates complete training with recognised skills and genuine employment opportunities.
### Apprenticeships and Internships
Young people need work experience before employers expect experience.
Structured apprenticeship and internship opportunities will therefore become a core component of the progression pathway.
Employers, GLCs and private companies will participate in preparing future talent instead of only recruiting experienced workers.
### Professional Entry Pathways
Graduates seeking careers in medicine, engineering, law, finance, ICT, education and other professional sectors should receive coordinated guidance, mentoring and placement support.
Talent should not be wasted because of weak professional networks.
### Civil Service Pathway
Public service should reflect Malaysia's diversity.
The Government will actively encourage qualified Malaysian Indian graduates to pursue careers in the civil service through targeted outreach, preparation programmes and transparent recruitment support.
The objective is to expand opportunities based on merit while ensuring capable candidates are aware of and prepared for public service careers.
### Leadership Development
Future community leaders should be developed long before they enter politics or public office.
Leadership programmes will focus on:
- public service;
- volunteerism;
- governance;
- entrepreneurship;
- innovation;
- and civic responsibility.
The next generation should be prepared not only to succeed personally but also to contribute nationally.
## What Young People Will Feel
Students will no longer graduate into uncertainty.
They will know where they are going.
Parents will gain confidence that education leads somewhere meaningful.
Graduates will experience stronger connections between education and employment.
The transition from school to adulthood becomes a guided journey rather than a gamble.
## What Success Looks Like
Within six years:
- more students enter higher education, TVET and professional careers;
- youth unemployment declines;
- apprenticeships become a normal bridge into employment;
- more graduates enter public service and leadership roles;
- and young Malaysians increasingly view their future as one of opportunity rather than limitation.
The second guarantee transforms education into mobility.
# Guarantee Three
# No Indian Family Stays Trapped in Low-Income Instability
## What This Guarantee Means
Economic security cannot be achieved through emergency assistance alone.
Families require stability.
Stability creates confidence.
Confidence encourages investment.
Investment creates ownership.
The objective is not to make families permanent recipients of aid.
The objective is to help families build resilience until government assistance is no longer required.
Success is achieved when households progress from vulnerability to self-reliance.
## What Government Will Do
The third guarantee focuses on creating long-term household stability.
### Household Progression Plans
Each participating family will have a coordinated progression pathway covering income, housing, education, entrepreneurship and financial wellbeing.
Support will be based on the family's overall circumstances rather than isolated problems.
### Income Stabilisation
Employment assistance, skills upgrading and entrepreneurship programmes will be coordinated to help households achieve stable and sustainable income.
The goal is not simply finding work.
It is achieving stable livelihoods.
### Housing Security
Affordable housing initiatives, rent-to-own opportunities and solutions for long-standing estate-related housing issues will be prioritised to help families build permanent security.
Home ownership remains one of the strongest foundations for long-term wealth.
### Microenterprise Growth
Many Malaysian Indian families already possess entrepreneurial ability.
The challenge is scaling businesses beyond survival.
Support will therefore extend beyond financing to include:
- mentoring;
- digitalisation;
- compliance;
- marketing;
- procurement access;
- export readiness;
- and business expansion.
Success is measured by businesses that grow—not merely businesses that start.
### Savings and Asset Growth
Financial resilience requires more than income.
Families should progressively build:
- emergency savings;
- investments;
- home ownership;
- productive business assets;
- and intergenerational wealth.
The Government's role is to help families move from short-term survival towards long-term financial security.
### Poverty Graduation
Households should not remain indefinitely within welfare systems.
Government assistance should help families graduate towards independence through measurable improvements in income, employment, housing and savings.
The question should never be:
"How long has this family received assistance?"
The better question is:
"How far has this family progressed?"
## What Families Will Feel
Families will experience greater financial stability.
Parents will feel more confident planning for their children's future.
Businesses will become stronger.
Savings will increase.
Housing security will improve.
Government support will feel connected rather than fragmented.
Families will begin to see themselves progressing rather than merely coping.
## What Success Looks Like
By the end of six years:
- more households achieve stable income;
- home ownership rises;
- more businesses expand beyond microenterprise level;
- savings and asset ownership increase;
- fewer families remain chronically dependent on welfare;
- and significantly more Malaysian Indian households move into the middle-income economy.
The third guarantee transforms government assistance into long-term prosperity.
# Three Guarantees. One National Promise.
Together, these guarantees create a progression model that is easy for every Malaysian to understand.
Every child starts with opportunity.
Every young person moves towards meaningful work.
Every family advances towards stability and ownership.
This is the promise at the heart of the MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees.
It is not a promise of endless assistance.
It is a promise that government will walk with families through every major stage of life until they no longer need help to move forward.
That is the difference between a collection of programmes and a national progression strategy.
That is the foundation upon which the next six years of Malaysian Indian advancement will be built.

# 4. First Two Years: Delivering Visible Change
## The First 24 Months Must Change What People See and Experience
Every long-term transformation begins by solving problems that people have lived with for far too long.
The first two years of the MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees are therefore designed around one principle:
Visible delivery builds public trust.
Families should not have to wait six years before experiencing change.
Within the first twenty-four months, Malaysians should be able to point to specific improvements that affect their daily lives.
A child finally receives documentation.
A qualified student secures a place in higher education.
A Tamil school roof is repaired.
A graduate joins the civil service.
A single mother receives support to grow her business.
A displaced family moves into secure housing.
A neglected temple becomes structurally safe and legally protected.
These are not abstract policy achievements.
They are tangible improvements that restore confidence in public institutions and demonstrate that government action produces real outcomes.
The first phase therefore concentrates on problems that are practical to solve, highly visible and capable of generating immediate public confidence.
# A. National Documentation and Identity Fast-Track Programme
## Every Malaysian-born Child and Family Must Have a Legal Identity
No Malaysian-born individual should spend years unable to access education, healthcare, employment or public services because of unresolved documentation.
The Government will launch a national fast-track initiative to resolve long-pending documentation and nationality cases involving Malaysian Indians.
Priority will be given to:
- children;
- students;
- elderly applicants;
- persons with disabilities;
- widows;
- single-parent households;
- and hardship cases.
Dedicated one-stop service centres will coordinate documentation support with mobile outreach teams serving estate communities, urban poor neighbourhoods and remote settlements.
Cases will no longer move endlessly between agencies without accountability.
Each application will receive active case management until resolution.
### Why It Matters Politically
Nothing demonstrates the presence of government more powerfully than giving a person their legal identity.
For affected families, this is not simply an administrative exercise.
It is recognition, dignity and inclusion.
### Visible Results Within Two Years
- Significant reduction in long-pending documentation cases.
- Faster processing times.
- Dedicated public reporting every month.
- Community confidence that longstanding problems are finally being addressed.
# B. Education Access and Opportunity Programme
## Every Qualified Student Deserves a Fair Opportunity
Educational opportunity should depend on ability and commitment—not on whether a student understands complex application systems.
The Government will establish a dedicated education progression support initiative to help Malaysian Indian students navigate:
- matriculation;
- public universities;
- polytechnics;
- community colleges;
- TVET institutions;
- scholarships;
- financial assistance;
- and professional education pathways.
Students will receive guidance from application through placement.
Qualified candidates should not lose opportunities because applications are incomplete, deadlines are missed or information is inaccessible.
### Why It Matters Politically
Parents judge governments by the opportunities available to their children.
Helping capable students enter higher education produces visible results that entire communities recognise.
### Visible Results Within Two Years
- Faster placement support.
- Greater transparency.
- Public reporting on admissions outcomes.
- Increased confidence that educational opportunities are accessible and fair.
# C. National Tamil School Renewal Programme
## Every Tamil School Must Be Safe, Modern and Ready for the Future
Tamil schools have educated generations of Malaysians and remain central to many communities.
The first responsibility is therefore not expansion.
It is ensuring that every existing school provides children with a safe and dignified learning environment.
A nationwide audit will assess every SJKT covering:
- structural safety;
- roofs;
- electrical wiring;
- sanitation;
- drainage;
- classrooms;
- accessibility;
- science facilities;
- digital infrastructure;
- internet connectivity;
- security;
- and maintenance requirements.
Repairs will be prioritised according to urgency rather than administrative sequence.
Each school's progress will be published through a publicly accessible implementation schedule.
### Why It Matters Politically
Communities quickly notice repaired schools.
Parents see improvements every morning.
Children experience them every day.
Visible infrastructure demonstrates that government investment reaches neighbourhoods rather than remaining inside reports.
### Visible Results Within Two Years
- Every SJKT audited.
- Safety-critical repairs underway or completed.
- Modern digital learning facilities expanded.
- Public repair schedules available for community monitoring.
# D. National Indian Civil Service Entry Initiative
## Opening More Doors into Public Service
The Malaysian civil service should attract talent from every part of Malaysian society.
The Government will introduce a dedicated outreach initiative encouraging qualified Malaysian Indian graduates and professionals to pursue careers in public service.
The initiative will include:
- career awareness campaigns;
- application guidance;
- examination preparation;
- interview readiness;
- mentorship by experienced officers;
- and structured follow-up throughout the recruitment process.
Recruitment remains merit-based.
The objective is ensuring that qualified candidates are fully aware of available opportunities and are well prepared to compete successfully.
### Why It Matters Politically
Public institutions should reflect the diversity of the nation.
Increasing participation strengthens confidence that opportunity is genuinely open to all Malaysians.
### Visible Results Within Two Years
- Higher application numbers.
- Greater interview participation.
- More successful appointments.
- Public reporting on recruitment outcomes.
# E. Household Economic Stability Programme
## Helping Families Move Beyond Daily Survival
Economic assistance should help households become more resilient—not merely manage recurring financial stress.
The Government will simplify access to:
- entrepreneurship financing;
- welfare support;
- microfinance;
- skills upgrading;
- business advisory services;
- financial counselling;
- and employment assistance.
Priority will be given to:

# 9. Delivery Architecture
## Turning Political Commitment into Visible Results
Every successful national initiative requires two elements.
A strong vision.
And a delivery system capable of turning that vision into measurable results.
Malaysia has never lacked policy ideas for the Malaysian Indian community.
The challenge has been ensuring that programmes introduced by different ministries, agencies and institutions operate as one coherent journey for families.
Too often, responsibility becomes fragmented.
Programmes overlap.
Cases move from one office to another.
Progress slows.
Citizens become frustrated.
Eventually, confidence declines—not because government lacks commitment, but because delivery appears inconsistent.
The MADANI Indian Progression Guarantees therefore introduce a delivery architecture that is deliberately simple.
Its purpose is not to create another bureaucracy.
Its purpose is to make government easier to access, easier to coordinate and easier to hold accountable.
The guiding principle is straightforward:
One family should experience one Government.
Not many governments operating independently.
# The Five Principles of Delivery
Every component of implementation should follow five simple principles.
## One Front Door
Families should know exactly where to begin.
Government assistance should not require citizens to understand organisational charts or navigate multiple agencies before receiving support.
Whether the issue involves:
- documentation;
- education;
- employment;
- entrepreneurship;
- housing;
- social assistance;
- or community development,
there should be a single, clearly identified point of entry.
The front door does not replace ministries.
It simplifies access.
Government becomes easier to approach.
Families spend less time searching for assistance and more time receiving it.
## One Family Profile
Government agencies often collect similar information repeatedly.
Families complete multiple forms.
Submit identical documents.
Repeat the same explanations.
This creates unnecessary delays.
The progression model introduces a single family profile that records the household's overall circumstances.
Rather than viewing each issue separately, Government develops a complete understanding of the family's progression journey.
The profile evolves as circumstances improve.
It reflects movement rather than static eligibility.
## One Progression Navigator
Families should never be left to coordinate multiple agencies alone.
Each participating household should have access to a designated Progression Navigator.
The Navigator's role is not to replace government agencies.
The role is to guide families through them.
Responsibilities include:
- understanding household needs;
- coordinating referrals;
- monitoring progress;
- identifying barriers;
- following up outstanding cases;
- connecting programmes together;
- and ensuring that families continue moving forward.
Instead of becoming another administrator, the Navigator becomes a facilitator of progress.
For many vulnerable households, knowing that one person is helping coordinate their journey dramatically reduces confusion and improves confidence.
## One Progression Pathway
Government support should resemble a connected pathway rather than isolated interventions.
Every completed stage should naturally lead to the next.
For example:
A child obtains documentation.
↓
The child enters preschool.
↓
The child begins primary school.
↓
Educational progress is monitored.
↓
Career guidance begins before school completion.
↓
The young person enters university, TVET or employment.
↓
Professional opportunities expand.
↓
Stable household income develops.
↓
Savings increase.
↓
Home ownership becomes achievable.
↓
Business growth or professional advancement follows.
↓
Long-term wealth begins to accumulate.
Every stage connects logically.
Families understand where they are going.
Government understands how to support the next step.
## One Public Dashboard
Public trust increases when citizens can see progress.
Delivery should therefore be measured openly.
The Government will publish a public Progress Dashboard tracking national outcomes across the three guarantees.
Examples include:
### Children
- documentation resolved;
- preschool enrolment;
- school readiness;
- Tamil school upgrading;
- educational access.
### Youth
- higher education placements;
- TVET participation;
- apprenticeships;
- employment outcomes;
- professional careers;
- civil service recruitment.
### Families
- household progression;
- entrepreneurship growth;
- housing outcomes;
- savings development;
- business expansion;
- movement into higher income categories.
The dashboard should communicate outcomes in language that ordinary Malaysians easily understand.
It should answer one question:
Are families moving forward?

Education remains responsible for education.
